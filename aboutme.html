About Me

My Journey Into Psychology & Medicine

My name is Hawa Kenneh, and I am a Psychology major in the Honors Carolina program at The University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, with an expected graduation year of 2030. I am from Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, where interacting with diverse individuals from various backgrounds has fueled my interest in medicine.

I was mentored by a nurse who minored in psychology, and she shared how her background allowed her to better understand her patients. She explained how psychology helped her notice subtle behaviors and emotional cues; things patients might not feel comfortable expressing. Because of this, she could provide more thoughtful, effective care.

Hearing this made me major in psychology. I was already interested before, but hearing this made me even more captivated by human perception and behavior. I was able to explore this fascination when I volunteered at Penn Presbyterian. I was placed in Family and Guest Relations for two days doing rotations with the head of the department. I closely observed his intentional approach to care and his ability to put patients at ease. Even when individuals were hesitant to open up, his genuine kindness completely transformed their comfort level.

Inspired by his example, I strive to bring that same warmth and attentiveness to everyone I interact with. While my time in Family and Guest Relations showed me the deep impact of daily and intentional care, my experience in the Emergency Room helped solidify my interest in medicine.

I watched a team of doctors, nurses, and technicians crowd a room to care for an elderly, unconscious patient. There was a lot of chaos, but what stood out to me was the deep compassion and urgency the staff showed this stranger while honoring the wishes of his family.

Witnessing that balance of clinical excellence and human empathy helped solidify my pursuit of medicine. As a Psychology student, I hope to continue learning about the connection between empathy and patient outcomes to better serve my patients in the future.
